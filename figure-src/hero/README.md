# README Hero Figure

Source of `assets/hero.png`, the image under the header of the project README. The master is `hero.pptx` (native PowerPoint objects, one slide, 13.333 x 6.15 in).

## Status

This is the first version, accepted by the maintainer on 2026-11-15 as a working figure that will likely be updated. Change it freely: edit `build_hero.py` (or `hero.pptx` by hand, then follow the rule below) and re-export `assets/hero.png`. An earlier matplotlib version (three text boxes, no visual objects) was rejected; keep the concrete objects (the configuration card, the step plate, the monitor, the dial, the magnifier, the leaderboard chart) when revising. After a change to the registry or the golden files, `tests/test_bench_readme.py` fails until the counts in `build_hero.py` and the README alt text are updated and the figure is rebuilt.

## Rebuild

```bash
python figure-src/hero/build_hero.py
```

The script writes `hero.pptx`, exports `hero.pdf` through desktop PowerPoint (macOS, AppleScript), and rasterizes `assets/hero.png` (2600 px wide, white background, 30 px of white margin on every side). It needs python-pptx 1.0.x, PyMuPDF, Pillow, and PowerPoint; `--no-export` writes only `hero.pptx`. Fonts are Arial for text and Consolas for code (both ship with Office).

`build_hero.py` regenerates the PPTX from scratch. If the PPTX is edited by hand in PowerPoint, treat that file as the master, export it again, and do not rerun the builder over it.

## Picture Boundary

The figure uses native text, autoshapes, connectors, and a bar chart, and it contains no embedded pictures. The PDF export may rasterize gradient fills, the hatched bars, and the transparent magnifier lens; the PPTX keeps them as native fills.

## Groups and Names

Shapes are named by role and grouped by region: `Banners`, `Plate`, `PRE`, `LIVE`, `POST`, `Leaderboards`, `Footer`. Inside `LIVE`, each step bead sits in its own group with its label (`step_0`, `step_1`, `step_2`, `step_t`), so moving that group moves the bead and its label together. Select a bead inside the group to move it alone.

## Connectors and Static Curves

Attached connectors (glued at both ends; a move made in the PowerPoint window re-routes them): the sequence links between the step beads, the link from step t to the monitor, and the arrow from the `run_shell` chip to step 0. A connector stays glued to a bead inside its step group. Moving a shape by editing the file with python-pptx does not re-route a connector; only a move in PowerPoint does.

Static shapes that need manual rerouting when a bead moves:

- the three dependency curves above the beads (`dep_curve_0_2`, `dep_curve_1_t`, `dep_curve_0_t`; freeform cubic Bezier curves with four editable points, using Edit Points)
- the alarm curve and its shaded area inside the monitor (custom geometry, cubic Bezier)
- the two fork arrows from the plate toward POST (freeform polygons)
- the dial segments and needle in POST (custom geometry)

## Chart and Data

The chart (`chart_detect_swegym_roc_auc`, a native clustered bar chart, editable through Edit Data) shows 6 of the 17 rows of the detect/swegym board. Values come from `src/auditable/bench/golden/detect__swegym.json` (`roc_auc` of `run.pygod_gaan`, `run.lr_spline_flat`, `run.gsafeguard_lite`, `run.lr_full`, `run.lr_flatdep`, `run.random`), read by the builder. The five highest rows plus `run.random` are drawn. Method bars use the POST coral, since detect/swegym is a POST board. The two controls (`run.lr_spline_flat` and `run.random`, both marked `(control)` in the project README) are hatched gray; `assets/leaderboard-swegym.png` hatches the same two controls in their Type colors. The hatch is a per-point override (a data point at a fixed index), so after reordering rows in Edit Data, reapply the hatch to the control rows. Long category names carry a line break inside the category cell.

The method counts, the leaderboard count, and the chart scores come from the registry and the golden data, and `tests/test_bench_readme.py` fails when a count in this builder drifts from the registry. Ranked steps (`#1 step 2` and so on), the needle position, the alarm curve, and the bead count are illustrative. Under the chart, `python -m auditable.bench` lists the six boards; `python -m auditable.bench <task>` reruns one.

## Known Deviations From the Brief

- The banners show two lines (`PRE` over `before the run`) instead of `PRE · before the run`.
- The canvas is 0.15 in taller than 6.0 in, which leaves air under the footer pill.
- Code boxes and board-id pills are 13 pt, and every other text run is 14 pt or larger. The monitor formula is 18 pt; its subscripts are 18 pt runs with a baseline of -25000, which PowerPoint draws at about 12 pt (the size the exported PDF reports).
